A chapter written with [Marius Ungarish](https://www.cs.technion.ac.il/) (Technion, Israel) and Vamsi Krishna Chalamalla (IIT Delhi) has appeared in _Particulate Gravity Currents: Theory, Experiments, and Environmental Applications_, published by Wiley in the AGU Geophysical Monograph Series.

Gravity currents in a stratified ambient are awkward to compare across studies. There are a lot of input parameters, and the problem gets worse for particle-driven flows, so different groups have scaled their data in different ways. Two approaches dominate the literature: empirical or data-processing analysis (EDP), and shallow-water (SW) theory. They use different definitions of the stratification parameter and different scalings for the variables, including the propagation speed itself, which makes it difficult to tell whether two sets of results actually disagree or are simply written differently.

In this chapter we take the slumping phase, where the current advances at a constant speed, and compare the two treatments directly for currents running along the bottom, along the top, and as mid-depth intrusions. We show that the SW method, with a small extension, is the more convenient of the two both physically and mathematically, and that it brings experiments, simulations and approximate models onto common ground for top and bottom currents alike, with and without particles.

The work grew out of our earlier _Physics of Fluids_ paper on [buoyant gravity currents propagating in a linearly stratified medium](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article-abstract/34/7/076605/2847554/Dynamics-of-a-buoyant-gravity-current-propagating), where we established the supercritical and subcritical regimes and a scaling for the front speed. That study dealt with currents along the top surface; the question of how those results sit alongside the much larger body of work on bottom currents is what this chapter sets out to answer.

It was a pleasure to work on this with Prof. Ungarish, whose shallow-water treatments of gravity currents a good deal of this analysis rests on, and the collaboration came together across three countries. You can read the chapter [here](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781394216727.ch6).
