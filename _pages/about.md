My research broadly focuses on environmental and geophysical fluid dynamics with a strong emphasis on turbulence which governs how these flows transport various quantities and mix them up. Such flows are generally non-uniform and transient in nature and the fluids involved are typically stratified. This leads to a competition between inertial and buoyancy effects which controls the underlying flow physics.

Most of my recent work has been on free shear flows, in particular on buoyant jets, turbulent fountains and plumes released into stratified and sheared environments. I have been using a combination of laboratory experiments, large-eddy simulations and reduced-order models to study them. I also have interest in wall-bounded turbulence, where I have spent some time conducting high-resolution measurements in a turbulent channel flow.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Assistant at the University of Oxford, building an experimental framework to study analogue volcanic plumes in laboratory. Before this, I completed my [PhD in Applied Mechanics]({{ '/news/phd_defense/' | relative_url }}) at IIT Delhi, where I investigated transport and mixing of turbulent fountains in stratified ambients. Earlier, I pursued an MPhil at the University of Melbourne and an MS at National Tsing Hua University, and completed my undergraduate degree at NIT Hamirpur, India.

Alongside research, I am passionate about teaching and am currently delivering open-access courses in fluid mechanics and computational methods on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@TanmayAgrawal7). I welcome connections and collaborations from fellow researchers and students alike.
