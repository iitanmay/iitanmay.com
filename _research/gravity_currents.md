A gravity current is a flow driven by a density (or buoyancy) difference between two fluids, which causes the denser fluid to spread horizontally along a surface. These flows are ubiquitous in nature and industry, from cold air spreading when you open a refrigerator door to large-scale phenomena like katabatic winds, undersea avalanches (turbidity currents), and volcanic pyroclastic flows.

The most fundamental setup to study them is the "lock-exchange" flow, where a barrier (or "lock") separating two fluids of different densities is suddenly removed. The denser fluid flows under the lighter one, and the lighter fluid flows over the denser one, creating two propagating "fronts."

MMy research focuses on the propagation dynamics of these currents, particularly when the surrounding (ambient) fluid is not uniform. In nature, the ocean or atmosphere is almost always _stratified_ (i.e., its density changes with depth or height). This stratification dramatically complicates the flow physics.

### Propagation Dynamics in a Stratified Environment

Using high-fidelity **Large Eddy Simulations (LES)**, we investigated buoyant (light) gravity currents propagating along the top surface of a linearly stratified medium. We found that the flow's behavior is controlled by a competition between the current's own buoyancy and the strength of the ambient stratification. This leads to two distinct flow regimes:

- **Supercritical Flow:** When the ambient stratification is weak, the current's front moves faster than the internal wave speed. This generates a prominent internal solitary wave that travels with the front, and we can observe large-scale Kelvin-Helmholtz billows.

- **Subcritical Flow:** When the ambient stratification is strong, the current is slower than the internal wave speed. The solitary wave and large billows disappear, and the flow's energy is instead radiated away by a field of vertically propagating, high-mode internal waves.

This work helped identify the physical mechanisms controlling the flow and led to the development of a new scaling law that accurately predicts the current's propagation speed.

### A Unified Theory for Stratified Flows

A major challenge in the field has been a lack of consistency. The analysis of stratified gravity currents is complex, with many competing physical parameters. Different studies have used different scaling methods—primarily **Empirical Data-Processing (EDP)** analysis versus **Shallow-Water (SW) theory**—making it difficult to compare results.

My recent review and theoretical work has focused on resolving this. We showed that the Shallow-Water (SW) theory is physically more convenient and can be extended to create a **unified framework**.

This unified framework is powerful because it consistently describes the propagation of _both_ top-propagating (buoyant) and bottom-propagating (dense) currents, with or without suspended particles. This generalization improves the insight and, most importantly, the **prediction power** for the constant-speed (or "slumping") phase of these complex flows.
